White hat · How were allegations against NYPD officers resolved, precinct by precinct?

The Civilian Complaint Review Board (CCRB) investigates complaints about NYPD officers. One complaint can contain several allegations (for example, a stop and a search), and each allegation gets its own outcome. Each bar below is one precinct, showing what share of its allegations were substantiated (the board found misconduct), exonerated (the conduct happened but was found to be within the law and NYPD rules), or unsubstantiated (not enough evidence to decide). Unsubstantiated does not mean the complaint was false. Precincts are the place where the incident happened. Hover or focus a bar for counts and complainant demographics.

Sorted by substantiation share, highest first. Scroll to see all precincts.

Coverage: fully investigated allegations involving officers serving in June 2020 with at least one substantiated allegation. This is not all NYPD activity. Demographics describe allegation records, not unique people or neighborhood populations. 2020 is incomplete.

Black hat · Complaints are less often substantiated in majority-Black precincts

Method: allegations received in 1995-2020 were grouped by the precinct where the incident occurred. Precincts with fewer than 100 allegations were excluded to limit small-sample noise. A precinct is labeled "Black" when more than half of its allegation records involve a Black complainant. Three precincts from each group are shown.

Source: ProPublica NYPD Files, CCRB data through July 2020.
